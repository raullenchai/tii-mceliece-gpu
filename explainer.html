Part 01 · The Problem in Plain English

1. McEliece & Binary Goppa Codes: A Geometric Lock Disguised as Noise

Unlike RSA and elliptic curves (which Shor's algorithm breaks on a quantum computer), the McEliece cryptosystem (1978) hides a structured binary Goppa error-correcting code behind a scrambled binary parity-check matrix H_pub. Classic McEliece reached Round 4 of NIST's post-quantum standardization (NIST selected HQC in March 2025) and remains one of the most conservative post-quantum designs.

The intuition is simple: take a precision algebraic lock and scramble its rows so it looks like random 0/1 noise.

What is the TII McEliece Track-2 Challenge?
The Technology Innovation Institute (TII) published 204 non-toy McEliece public-key recovery instances (plus 2 toy checks): given only H_pub, recover an equivalent private key (g, L) whose Goppa parity-check matrix has the exact same row space and passes check_solution_track2.sage with output 1.
Part 02 · The 2026 Escalation Ladder ("Boss-Fight Progression")

2. Climbing the Ladder: From mt = 63 to mt = 144 (and the Wall Beyond)

One thing trips up almost every newcomer to TII Track-2: why is TII-235 much harder than TII-237 even though 235 < 237?
TII's challenge IDs were estimated in 2023 under older attacks. Under 2026 Primal-Shortened ISD, the true "boss level" is governed primarily by the codimension mt = m × t (each +9 bits multiplies search space by roughly 4–5×) and the shortened dimension k' = n' - mt.

Part 03 · How It Works Under the Hood

3. Two-Stage Relay: L2-Resident GPU Harvesting + 10–25 s Algebraic Lift

We keep MiniMax's algebraic polar-lifting algorithm and Mode-6 Stern parameters (p = 3+3, l = 17, 16 KB occupancy bitmap) untouched. Why? Because at mt ≥ 108, over 99% of total runtime is spent harvesting minimum odd-weight (w = 2t + 1) codewords, while SageMath finishes the algebraic lift in 10–25 seconds.

Four Coupled GPU-Aware Modifications

1. Lossless 16-Byte Zero-Index Packing

The 17-bit bucket index fixes the low 17 syndrome bits. For any mt ≤ 145, all remaining mt − 17 ≤ 128 bits fit losslessly into one 16-byte ulonglong2. Because the weight-filter pass probability is tiny (2^-27.02 to 2^-35.74), we drop left-triple indices from the hot bucket table and reconstruct them only on exact hits.

2. Cooperative L2-Sized Tables (G = 4 / G = 8)

Assigning one hash table per SM allocates 1.04–1.49 GB and thrashes global memory. Instead, all SMs cooperate on G=4 tables on L4 (35.7 MB < 50.3 MB L2) or G=8 tables on L40S (71.4 MB < 100.7 MB L2, 144 cooperative blocks).

3. Batched Full-SM Systematic Reduction

CUDA event profiling on v3 revealed that unbatched Gaussian elimination (k_sys<<<4,256>>>) consumed 74.0% of runtime while leaving 54/58 SMs idle. Batching 64G random reductions per launch (256 on L4, 512 on L40S) raised L4 throughput 3.5× to 9,462 it/s on TII-237 and 11,035 it/s on TII-245.

4. GPU-Aware Optimal Shortening Window n'

Narrow CPU-oriented prefixes hit coupon-collector duplication late in the run. Widening n' (TII-228: 395, TII-235: 425) keeps SageMath M4RI under 25 s while cutting modeled GPU-hours by 34%–47% and holding 10-GPU cross-worker overlap to 2.06%.
