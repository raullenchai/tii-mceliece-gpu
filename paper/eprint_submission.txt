TITLE:
Packing Stern into L2: GPU-Accelerated Primal-Shortened ISD and Key Recovery up to TII-235 (mt=144)

AUTHORS:
Raullen Chai (MachineFi Labs)
Xinxin Fan (MachineFi Labs)

CONTACT EMAIL:
raullen@machinefi.com, xinxin@machinefi.com

CATEGORY:
Public-Key Cryptography

KEYWORDS:
McEliece cryptosystem, binary Goppa codes, information-set decoding, GPU cryptanalysis, TII McEliece Challenges

ABSTRACT (plain text, ready for web form):
MiniMax recently showed that Primal-Shortened Information-Set Decoding (ISD) with quadratic polar lifting recovers keys from high-codimension TII McEliece Track-2 challenges, reaching mt = 126 on TII-228 in 2,463 CPU core-hours (57.1 min across 2,048-5,120 EPYC cores). Because more than 99% of runtime at mt >= 108 goes to harvesting minimum odd-weight (w = 2t + 1) codewords of a shortened parity-check matrix H_A, the algebraic lift is no longer the limiting stage. We ask how far this harvesting bottleneck can be pushed on GPUs without altering the algebraic recovery.

Porting MiniMax's Mode-6 Stern sampler (p = 3 + 3, l = 17) directly to one block per SM leaves 1.0-1.5 GB of hash tables active and stalls on global memory. For mt <= 145, we pack all mt - 17 <= 128 remaining syndrome bits into one 16-byte ulonglong2 without storing left-triple indices, cooperate across all SMs on G concurrent tables sized below nominal L2 capacity (G = 4, 35.7 MB on L4; G = 8, 71.4 MB on L40S), and batch 64G random systematic reductions per launch (256 on L4, 512 on L40S) after profiling exposed that unbatched elimination left 54/58 SMs idle for 74.0% of runtime. Under a Poisson-bucket and coupon-collector model, widening the shortening prefix n' beyond narrow CPU-oriented choices reduces total GPU-hours by 34%-47% while keeping SageMath linear algebra under 25 s.

We validate the complete pipeline across four Track-2 challenges spanning mt = 108 to 144, passing both the official SageMath checker and a standalone pure-Python verifier on every instance. On TII-245 (mt = 108), one NVIDIA L4 finishes in 1,185.3 s harvesting plus lift (~1.91x target-normalized over a 64-core Xeon). On TII-228 (mt = 126), eight Modal L40S GPUs recover the key in 18.9 min wall-clock (2.15 GPU-hours, 93.6M iterations, 24.64 s lift), outperforming MiniMax's multi-thousand-core EPYC run by 3.0x in wall-clock time. On the previously undocumented instances TII-237 (mt = 135, 22.1 Colab L4 GPU-hours) and TII-235 (n = 502, m = 9, t = 16, mt = 144, 4.30 h wall-clock across ten Modal L40S GPUs, 39.62 GPU-hours, 17.97 s lift), we recover equivalent private keys using 127 of 128 bits—within one bit of the lossless packing ceiling (mt = 145). To our knowledge as of November 22, 2026, mt = 144 is the largest codimension among publicly documented Track-2 solutions. These results do not demonstrate practical recovery against proposed Classic McEliece parameter sets (mt >= 768).
