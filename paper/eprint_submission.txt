TITLE:
Packing Stern into L2: GPU-Accelerated Primal-Shortened ISD and Key Recovery on TII-237 (mt=135)

AUTHORS:
Raullen Qi Chai (MachineFi Labs)

CONTACT EMAIL:
raullen@machinefi.com

CATEGORY:
Public-Key Cryptography

KEYWORDS:
McEliece cryptosystem, binary Goppa codes, information-set decoding, GPU cryptanalysis, TII McEliece Challenges

ABSTRACT (plain text, ready for web form):
MiniMax recently showed that Primal-Shortened Information-Set Decoding (ISD) with quadratic polar lifting recovers keys from high-codimension TII McEliece Track-2 challenges, reaching mt = 126 on TII-228 in 2,463 CPU core-hours. On instances with mt >= 108, over 99% of runtime goes to harvesting minimum odd-weight (w = 2t + 1) codewords of a shortened parity-check matrix H_A. We ask whether that harvesting bottleneck moves cleanly from CPU clusters to GPUs without changing the algebraic attack.

A per-SM GPU port of MiniMax's Mode-6 Stern sampler (p = 3 + 3, l = 17) keeps 1.0-1.5 GB of hash tables active and stalls on global memory. For mt <= 145, we pack all mt - 17 <= 128 remaining syndrome bits into one 16-byte ulonglong2 without storing left-triple indices, cooperate across all SMs on G = 4 concurrent tables (35.7 MB total, below the 50.3 MB L4 and 41.9 MB A100 L2 caches), and batch 256 random systematic reductions per launch after profiling showed unbatched elimination spent 74.0% of runtime with 54/58 SMs idle.

On one NVIDIA L4, the kernel sustains 11,035 it/s on TII-245 and 9,462 it/s on TII-237 (3.1x and 4.3x over our per-SM port). On TII-245 (mt = 108), a single L4 recovers an equivalent private key in 1,185.3 s end-to-end (2.12x faster than MiniMax's 64-core Xeon run, or ~1.9x normalized for target size). Across ~22.1 Colab L4 GPU-hours (~7.4e8 iterations), we harvest 29,742 weight-31 codewords for TII-237 (n = 500, m = 9, t = 15, mt = 135) and recover an equivalent private key in 23.23 s of SageMath post-processing—to our knowledge as of November 22, 2026, the largest codimension among publicly documented Track-2 solutions. These experiments do not demonstrate practical recovery against proposed Classic McEliece parameter sets (mt >= 768).
