\begin{abstract}
MiniMax recently showed that Primal-Shortened Information-Set Decoding (ISD) with quadratic polar lifting recovers keys from high-codimension TII McEliece Track-2 challenges, reaching $mt=126$ on \texttt{TII-228} in $2{,}463$ CPU core-hours. On instances with $mt \ge 108$, over $99\%$ of runtime goes to harvesting minimum odd-weight ($w=2t+1$) codewords of a shortened parity-check matrix $H_A$. We ask whether that harvesting bottleneck moves cleanly from CPU clusters to GPUs without changing the algebraic attack.

A per-SM GPU port of MiniMax's Mode-6 Stern sampler ($p=3+3, l=17$) keeps $1.0\text{--}1.5\text{ GB}$ of hash tables active and stalls on global memory. For $mt \le 145$, we pack all $mt-17 \le 128$ remaining syndrome bits into one $16$-byte \texttt{ulonglong2} without storing left-triple indices, cooperate across all SMs on $G=4$ concurrent tables ($35.7\text{ MB}$ total, below the $50.3\text{ MB}$ L4 and $41.9\text{ MB}$ A100 L2 caches), and batch $256$ random systematic reductions per launch after profiling showed unbatched elimination spent $74.0\%$ of runtime with $54/58$ SMs idle.

On one NVIDIA L4, the kernel sustains $11{,}035\text{ it/s}$ on \texttt{TII-245} and $9{,}462\text{ it/s}$ on \texttt{TII-237} ($3.1\times$ and $4.3\times$ over our per-SM port). On \texttt{TII-245} ($mt=108$), a single L4 recovers an equivalent private key in $1{,}185.3\text{ s}$ end-to-end ($2.12\times$ faster than MiniMax's $64$-core Xeon run, or $\approx 1.9\times$ normalized for target size). Across $\approx 22.1$ Colab L4 GPU-hours ($\approx 7.4\times 10^8$ iterations), we harvest $29{,}742$ weight-$31$ codewords for \texttt{TII-237} ($n=500, m=9, t=15, mt=135$) and recover an equivalent private key in $23.23\text{ s}$ of SageMath post-processing---to our knowledge as of November 22, 2026, the largest codimension among publicly documented Track-2 solutions. These experiments do not demonstrate practical recovery against proposed Classic McEliece parameter sets ($mt \ge 768$).

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} McEliece cryptosystem, binary Goppa codes, information-set decoding, GPU cryptanalysis, TII McEliece Challenges.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The McEliece cryptosystem~\cite{McEliece78} hides a binary Goppa code $\Gamma(L, g)$ behind a systematic parity-check matrix $H_{\mathrm{pub}} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{mt \times n}$. Classic McEliece reached Round~4 of the NIST post-quantum standardization process (NIST selected HQC in March 2025). To measure concrete structural key-recovery margins across code dimensions and rates, the Technology Innovation Institute (TII) published $204$ non-toy Track-2 public-key recovery instances (plus two toy instances in the checker)~\cite{TIIRepo}.

In 2026, algebraic and hybrid attacks pushed Track-2 solutions far past earlier bounds. Bardet, Lemoine, and Tillich~\cite{BLT26} reached \texttt{TII-210} ($mt=63$) via Pfaffian relations; Hemmert~\cite{Hemmert26} reached \texttt{TII-248} ($mt=63$) with higher-order vanishing (HOV); Vedenev~\cite{Vedenev26} and Weis~\cite{Weis26} solved \texttt{TII-253} ($mt=72$); and Saarinen pushed Frobenius-reduced HOVER to \texttt{TII-252} ($mt=110$)~\cite{Saarinen26HOVER} and Two-Anchor Holdout/Hermite to \texttt{TII-254} ($mt=96$, reporting $27.2$ GPU-hours for the two final Krylov sequences on $8\times$ NVIDIA GH200)~\cite{Saarinen26TwoAnchor}. On November 20, 2026, MiniMax~\cite{MiniMax26} introduced Primal-Shortened ISD with quadratic polar lifting (Route~A) alongside an optimized HOVER path (Route~B), recovering $94$ Track-2 keys ($55$ new) up to \texttt{TII-228} ($mt=126$, $2{,}463$ physical CPU core-hours in $57.1\text{ min}$ on $2{,}048\text{--}5{,}120$ EPYC cores) and \texttt{TII-250} ($mt=120$, $66{,}722$ core-hours on $5{,}120\text{--}10{,}240$ cores).

What makes Route~A unusual among structural attacks is its cost split. On \texttt{TII-245} ($mt=108$), the final algebraic lift in SageMath takes under ten seconds; more than $99\%$ of runtime is spent inside a single OpenMP C sampler (\texttt{minwords\_mt.c}) harvesting weight-$(2t+1)$ codewords of a shortened parity-check matrix $H_A$. Codeword harvesting therefore dominates the measured recovery time.

This note leaves MiniMax's algebraic recovery algorithm, Mode-6 Stern parameters ($p=3+3, l=17$, $16\text{ KB}$ occupancy bitmap), and shortening schedule (\path{optimal_shortening_sizes}) intact, and re-engineers only the codeword harvester for GPU memory hierarchies. A single NVIDIA L4 outperforms a $64$-core Xeon server by $\approx 1.9\times$ (target-normalized) on \texttt{TII-245}, and $\approx 22.1$ aggregate Colab L4 GPU-hours suffice to harvest $29{,}742$ weight-$31$ codewords and recover an equivalent private key for \texttt{TII-237} ($n=500, m=9, t=15, mt=135$), which to our knowledge had no publicly documented solution as of November 22, 2026. Because $mt$ alone does not order instance difficulty across different $(n, m, t, n')$ tuples---indeed, \texttt{TII-237} has smaller shortened dimension $k'=243$ than \texttt{TII-250}'s $k'=347$---we treat $mt=135$ strictly as a codimension milestone, not a claim that \texttt{TII-237} is harder than every lower-$mt$ challenge.

\section{Primal-Shortened ISD}

Let $L = (\alpha_0, \dots, \alpha_{n-1}) \in \mathbb{F}_{2^m}^n$ be an ordered support of $n$ distinct elements and $g(X) \in \mathbb{F}_{2^m}[X]$ a monic square-free Goppa polynomial of degree $t$ with $g(\alpha_i) \ne 0$ for all $i$. Let $H_{\mathrm{pub}} \in \mathbb{F}_2^{mt \times n}$ be the systematic parity-check matrix of $\Gamma(L, g)$ (with designed redundancy $mt$; we check full rank empirically on each instance). Fixing a prefix window $A = \{0, \dots, n'-1\}$ with $\operatorname{rank}(H_A) = mt$ defines the shortened Goppa code $\mathcal{C}_A = \ker(H_A) = \Gamma(L_A, g)$ of dimension $k' = n' - mt$.

Any codeword $c \in \mathcal{C}_A$ of minimum odd Hamming weight $w = 2t + 1$ and support $S \subset A$ has locator polynomial $\sigma_S(X) = \prod_{i \in S}(X - \alpha_i)$ satisfying the characteristic-$2$ derivative-square identity $\sigma'_S(X) = g(X)^2$ (credited to Finiasz in~\cite{MiniMax26}). Restricting to the nonempty odd-weight affine slice of $\mathcal{C}_A$ (affine dimension $d = k' - 1$), MiniMax~\cite{MiniMax26} showed that every weight-$(2t+1)$ codeword satisfies $m+1$ quadratic relations over the $F(d) = 1 + d + \binom{d}{2}$ quadratic monomials in $d$ coordinates. One relation is the public quadratic weight identity $\sum_{i<j} c_i c_j = \binom{w}{2} \bmod 2$, whose associated symmetric bilinear polar form we denote $T_{\mathrm{pub}}$.

Under the heuristic hypothesis that the harvested weight-$(2t+1)$ codewords produce an evaluation matrix of rank $F(d) - (m+1)$ (making $F(d)-(m+1)$ a planning threshold rather than a guarantee), dense $\mathbb{F}_2$ elimination recovers the $(m+1)$-dimensional relation space. Modding out $T_{\mathrm{pub}}$ leaves $m$ polar forms whose lifting yields an equivalent pair $(L_A, g)$. Once the unscrambling transformation is computed and applied to $H_{\mathrm{pub}}$, extending $L_A$ to all $n$ columns takes $O(n)$ field divisions for fixed $(m, t)$.

For \texttt{TII-237} ($n=500, m=9, t=15, mt=135, w=31$), MiniMax's shortening schedule selects $n'=378$, giving measured $\operatorname{rank}(H_A)=135$, $k'=243$, $d=242$, and planning threshold $F(d)-(m+1)=29{,}394$. A random-code heuristic predicts $\binom{378}{31}/2^{135} \approx 62{,}943$ weight-$31$ codewords in $\mathcal{C}_A$. Lincoln--Petersen capture--recapture between our two largest independent-seed L4 workers ($|A|=17{,}009$, $|B|=8{,}570$, $|A \cap B|=2{,}215$) yields $\hat{N} = 65{,}809 \pm 1{,}123$ (standard error; 95\% CI $[63{,}608, 68{,}010]$), within $5\%$ of the random-code prediction. Both figures are heuristic and subject to the sampling caveats in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\section{Fitting Stern into the GPU L2 Cache}

MiniMax's Mode-6 CPU sampler performs full random systematic reduction followed by a Stern-style~\cite{Stern89,Dumer91} meet-in-the-middle search with $p=3+3$ non-pivot bits, $l=17$ collision bits ($2^{17} = 131{,}072$ buckets), and a $16\text{ KB}$ occupancy bitmap pre-filter (referred to as Stern--Dumer following~\cite{MiniMax26}). Each reduction splits the $k'$ non-pivot columns into left and right halves of sizes $\lfloor k'/2 \rfloor$ and $\lceil k'/2 \rceil$---for \texttt{TII-237}, $121$ and $122$ columns, yielding $\binom{121}{3}=287{,}980$ left triples and $\binom{122}{3}=295{,}240$ right triples.

On a CPU, one thread owns one reduction and its hash table fits in L2/L3 cache. Moving that design directly to a GPU does not work. Our per-SM port (\texttt{v1}: $58$ blocks, $6$ slots $\times$ $32$ bytes per bucket, $25.7\text{ MB}$ per table) keeps $1.49\text{ GB}$ of hash tables live. Halving each entry to $16$ bytes with $4$ slots and raising occupancy to $2$ blocks/SM (\texttt{v2}: $116$ blocks, $8.93\text{ MB}$ per table, $1.04\text{ GB}$ total) reduced aggregate allocation yet lowered \texttt{TII-237} throughput from $2{,}208$ to $1{,}872\text{ it/s}$: both allocations exceed the $50.3\text{ MB}$ L4 L2 cache by more than $20\times$, and twice as many blocks contend for it.

Our final kernel (\path{src/minwords_gpu_v4.cu}, derived from MiniMax's \texttt{minwords\_mt.c} under Apache-2.0) addresses this in three steps.

\paragraph{Lossless 16-byte packing without left indices.}
The bucket index $h \in [0, 2^{17})$ already fixes the lowest $17$ syndrome bits. Whenever $mt \le 145$, the remaining $mt - 17 \le 128$ bits fit losslessly into two $64$-bit words:
\[
  p_0 = (s_0 \gg 17) \mid (s_1 \ll 47), \qquad p_1 = (s_1 \gg 17) \mid (s_2 \ll 47).
\]
We store only $(p_0, p_1)$ as a $16$-byte \texttt{ulonglong2} and omit left-triple indices $(a, b, c)$ from the hot bucket array. Given a $17$-bit bucket collision, a uniform-syndrome heuristic puts the conditional weight-filter pass probability $\binom{mt-17}{w-6}/2^{mt-17}$ at $2^{-27.02}$ for \texttt{TII-245} and $2^{-33.57}$ for \texttt{TII-237}. Because the packing is lossless for $mt \le 145$, every exact match ($\operatorname{popc}(p_0 \oplus r_{p_0}) + \operatorname{popc}(p_1 \oplus r_{p_1}) = w - 6$) is a genuine weight-$w$ codeword of the systematic matrix; only on these rare hits does the thread unpack $(s_0, s_1, s_2)$ and recover $(a, b, c)$ from shared-memory \texttt{colmask}. With $4$ slots per bucket, each bucket payload is $64$ bytes aligned ($8.39\text{ MB}$/table, alongside a separate $0.52\text{ MB}$ count array and $16\text{ KB}$ bitmap). Under a Poisson load model, the $4$-slot cap retains $\approx 98.03\%$ of left triples on \texttt{TII-245} ($\lambda \approx 1.60$) and $\approx 95.13\%$ on \texttt{TII-237} ($\lambda \approx 2.20$, discarding $\approx 4.87\%$ in atomic arrival order).

\paragraph{Cooperative execution sized for L2 ($G=4$).}
Rather than one table per SM, we allocate $G=4$ concurrent tables whose bucket payloads, counts, and bitmaps occupy $35{,}717{,}120$ bytes ($35.7\text{ MB}$ / $34.1\text{ MiB}$)---below the L2 cache capacities of NVIDIA L4 ($50.3\text{ MB}$) and A100 ($41.9\text{ MB}$). Sixteen blocks ($4{,}096$ threads) cooperate per group during \texttt{k\_build} and \texttt{k\_probe}, caching the $16\text{ KB}$ occupancy bitmap in shared memory per block.

\paragraph{Batched systematic reduction.}
Moving from \texttt{v2} to the cooperative $G=4$ kernel (\texttt{v3}) lifted \texttt{TII-237} throughput to only $2{,}671\text{ it/s}$. CUDA event profiling over $256$ iterations on L4 showed why:
\[
  \texttt{k\_sys}: 68.00\text{ ms}~(74.0\%), \quad
  \texttt{k\_zero}: 0.34\text{ ms}, \quad
  \texttt{k\_build}: 10.82\text{ ms}, \quad
  \texttt{k\_probe}: 12.72\text{ ms}.
\]
The bucket build and probe were already fast ($23.54\text{ ms}$ combined), but launching \texttt{k\_sys<<<4, 256>>>} left $54$ of $58$ L4 SMs idle during random systematic reduction. Batching $256$ independent reductions in one \texttt{k\_sys<<<256, 256>>>} launch ($2.2\text{ MB}$ state buffer) ahead of $64$ cooperative build/probe steps raised single-L4 throughput $3.5\times$ to $9{,}462\text{ it/s}$ on \texttt{TII-237} and $3.17\times$ to $11{,}035\text{ it/s}$ on \texttt{TII-245}.

In single-run measurements on \texttt{TII-237}, one NVIDIA L4 ($50.3\text{ MB}$ L2, $2.04\text{ GHz}$ boost clock, $1.94\text{ CU/hr}$ on Colab as of Nov.\ 2026) achieved $9{,}462\text{ it/s}$, compared to $8{,}035\text{ it/s}$ on an A100-SXM4-40GB ($41.9\text{ MB}$ L2, $1.41\text{ GHz}$ boost clock, $\approx 6.8\text{ CU/hr}$)---$1.18\times$ faster in iteration rate and $>4\times$ higher in iterations per compute unit.

\section{Limitations and Artifacts}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{Cache measurements and variance.}
Sizing the $35.7\text{ MB}$ table footprint below L2 capacity does not prove $100\%$ L2 hit rates without hardware counter profiling (e.g., Nsight Compute). Table~\ref{tab:evolution} and the A100 figure are single-run measurements without $G \in \{2, 4, 8\}$ ablations.

\paragraph{Sampling bias and non-determinism.}
Non-pivot columns are split in sorted order rather than shuffled per reduction, which biases sampling; $4$-slot buckets discard $\approx 4.87\%$ of left triples on \texttt{TII-237} in atomic arrival order, which makes per-iteration output non-deterministic. Both effects make the Lincoln--Petersen supply figure a heuristic.

\paragraph{Domain limits and post-run hardening.}
\path{src/minwords_gpu_v4.cu} enforces $mt \le 145$, $k' \le 320$, and $\texttt{mode}=6$ (rejecting wider instances such as \texttt{TII-250} with $k'=347$). After our harvesting runs, we hardened host-side validation, checkpoint persistence, and \texttt{k\_sys} RNG initialization and retry bounds while leaving the \texttt{k\_build} and \texttt{k\_probe} search kernels unchanged.

\paragraph{Key equivalence and leaderboard status.}
The witnesses certify \emph{equivalent} private keys $(g, L)$ generating $H_{\mathrm{pub}}$ bit-for-bit, not necessarily the originally sampled generator representation. \texttt{TII-237} has not yet been acknowledged on the official TII leaderboard.

\paragraph{Artifacts.}
All code, witnesses, compressed codeword datasets, and run manifests are publicly available at \url{https://github.com/raullenchai/tii-mceliece-gpu} under Apache-2.0 (preserving MiniMax's upstream notices in \path{LICENSE} and \path{NOTICE}). Running
\begin{center}
\texttt{python3 src/verify\_witnesses.py -{}-check-words}
\end{center}
checks both equivalent private keys, hardcoded SHA-256 digests, and all $24{,}500 + 29{,}742$ harvested codewords in pure Python with no external dependencies, while \path{src/solve_from_words.py} reproduces the algebraic recovery directly from the published codeword files (runtime depends on the host CPU).

\section{Conclusion}

Packing MiniMax's Mode-6 Stern harvester into a GPU's L2 cache budget moves high-codimension Primal-Shortened ISD from CPU clusters to GPUs. On one NVIDIA L4, \texttt{minwords\_gpu\_v4} sustains $9{,}462\text{--}11{,}035\text{ it/s}$, and $\approx 22.1$ Colab L4 GPU-hours suffice to recover an equivalent private key for \texttt{TII-237} ($mt=135$). These experiments do not demonstrate practical key recovery at proposed Classic McEliece parameter sets ($m \ge 12, t \ge 64, mt \ge 768$).
